Verdict

There IS enough content to extract brand guidelines. The site has a strong, consistent visual identity and a well-defined voice. What's missing is formalization — the brand rules exist implicitly in the CSS and content, but they're not documented as a referenceable system.

Below is what we found, what's strong, and what needs to be built.

Brand Coverage Assessment

How much of a complete brand system is already present in the codebase?

Color Palette Strong

11 colors defined as CSS variables. Clear primary (black/white/yellow), secondary (lavender/purple), and neutral scale.

Typography Strong

2 distinctive fonts (Syncopate + Instrument Sans), full type scale, 3 line heights, weight system. All uppercase headings.

Spacing System Strong

11-step spacing scale from xxs (0.25rem) to 5xl (8rem). Container, radius, and shadow tokens.

Voice & Tone Strong

Consistent across 15 pages and 5 blog posts. Casual, bold, playful, authentic. Clear mantra: "Fresh looks, bold moves."

Component Library Strong

11 reusable Astro components with consistent patterns. Buttons, cards, heroes, galleries, tabs, FAQs, CTAs.

Imagery Direction Implicit

80 images with clear themes (lifestyle, social, fashion) but no documented photography guidelines or art direction rules.

Brand Story / Mission Implicit

Implied through content tone, but no formal mission statement, brand values, or positioning statement exists.

Logo Guidelines Missing

SVG icon exists + text wordmark, but no usage rules, clear space, minimum sizes, or color variants documented.

What's Missing for Enterprise-Ready Guidelines

Logo System

The SVG icon and "Fashion Blog" wordmark exist but have no usage rules. Need: clear space, minimum sizes, color variants (black, white, yellow), lockup options, and forbidden treatments.

Brand Story & Positioning

No formal mission statement, brand values, or positioning document. The voice pillars are implicit — they need to be codified so any content creator (human or AI) can follow them.

Photography & Art Direction

80 images follow a clear direction (lifestyle, diversity, energy) but there are no documented rules for: image style, color treatment, composition, model demographics, do's and don'ts.

Tone Spectrum for Different Contexts

Voice is consistent but there's no guidance for how tone shifts across contexts: marketing vs. support vs. error messages vs. social media. Enterprise brands need this.

Content Patterns & Templates

Blog posts follow a consistent structure, but it's not documented. Need: headline formulas, paragraph length rules, CTA placement conventions, image-to-text ratios.

Social Media Guidelines

5 social platforms linked (Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, YouTube) but no documented approach for each platform's voice, content format, or posting style.

Proposed Approach: Brand Guidelines System

How to turn what exists into an enterprise-ready, AI-enforceable brand system.

1. Create brand/ directory

A structured set of markdown files that codify the brand. Machine-readable by Claude, human-readable for any team member.

FileContents
brand/identity.mdMission, values, positioning, brand story, audience
brand/voice.mdVoice pillars, tone spectrum, do/don't examples, vocabulary
brand/visual.mdColors, typography, spacing, shapes, shadows, motion
brand/logo.mdLogo usage, clear space, variants, forbidden treatments
brand/photography.mdImage style, subjects, composition, color treatment, diversity
brand/content.mdHeadline formulas, content patterns, CTA conventions, SEO rules
brand/social.mdPer-platform guidelines, content format, posting voice

2. Reference from CLAUDE.md

Add one line: "Follow brand guidelines in brand/ for all content and design work." This makes the brand system enforceable by Claude across all future conversations.

3. Generate from existing content

Most of the content for these files already exists — it just needs to be extracted and formalized. The CSS variables become the visual spec. The site copy becomes the voice examples. The image library becomes the photography direction.

Bottom Line

The brand is already there. It's living in the code. What's needed is extraction and formalization into a brand/ directory that any future Claude conversation can read and enforce. This is the equivalent of what enterprise CMS platforms store in their "brand center" or "design system" modules — but it's 7 markdown files instead of a licensed platform.
